Forte · design options

Duplas & the rest dock

Two questions from the gym floor. One: the program has exercises that are meant to be paired — overhead press and chin-ups, alternating with ~1:30 between — and pairing isn't in the design at all. Two: the idle rest dock highlights the suggested tier with solid rose, which makes the left pill look permanently active. Three shapes for the first, four for the second.

Part 1 · Paired exercises
Where does the pairing live?

Forte has no per-set logging — a slot is one card, one ring, one weight. So a pair is a small thing to say: these two alternate, rest 1:30 between sets. The three options say it in three different places: on a shared mat, inside one card, or in the rest pill itself. Data-wise all three are the same tiny change — a shared pair id on two adjacent slots. Copy note: the tag says Dupla — pair, in Carolina's Portuguese — swap for “Pair” if too cute.

The rose mat

Both cards stay exactly as they are — they just sit together on one tinted mat with a single tag. The lightest possible touch.

Read on part 1

These compose. A or B is the map (see the pair while planning the station), C is the turn-by-turn (know what's next while resting). The strongest build is probably B + C's rest pill — structural card, guided rest — with A alone as the honest cheap first step if you want to ship something this week. All three want the same seed change: a shared pair id on two adjacent slots, rest between = the normal tier.

Part 2 · The rest dock
Nothing is “on” before she presses

Diagnosis. restDockHTML() paints the suggested tier solid rose and mutes the other. But solid rose is Forte's selected state everywhere else — rungs, segments, pips — so the filled pill reads as “already running.” Since most slots rest normal, that's the left pill nearly all session: a stuck-looking toggle. And when a heavy slot comes up, the fill jumps sides — which reads as state that changed by itself, not advice. Each variant below is shown in both suggestion states, so you can check that nothing appears to flip on its own.

Even hands

Two roses

Read on part 2

My lean: Variant 2 — it fixes the misread at its root (asymmetric paint) with the smallest diff, and the dock keeps being the day view's one warm action zone. Variant 3 is the more radical candidate if you're willing to demote the tier choice; it's the most on-brand but re-imports a small flip and hides an option the two-tier design chose on purpose. V1 and V4 both solve the ambiguity fully but pay for it by muting the dock — V4 is the better of those two if pairing lands, since its caption line is also a natural home for “dupla — then Chin-ups.”

All four keep state on luminance, not hue — solid rose vs. matte shell — so nothing changes for color-blind eyes checking “what's pressed.”
